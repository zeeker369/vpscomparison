Why China Routing Still Matters in 2026

If your VPS serves users in mainland China — or you’re a developer in China connecting to overseas infrastructure — network selection is one of the most consequential decisions you’ll make. Standard international routes get hammered at peak hours. Packet loss climbs. Latency becomes unpredictable.

China’s network environment hasn’t gotten simpler. In 2026, commodity routes are showing tighter congestion patterns while premium route pricing has shifted again. CN2 GIA costs have risen, pushing more developers toward AS9929 as a genuine alternative. AS4837, meanwhile, remains the go-to for high-bandwidth, budget-conscious deployments.

This guide skips the preamble and gets to what matters: which network fits your use case, and what you’re actually paying for.

What These Networks Actually Are

CN2 GIA Explained

CN2 stands for China Telecom Next Carrier Network. GIA stands for Global Internet Access. CN2 GIA is the premium tier of China Telecom’s international backbone — distinct from, and significantly better than, the cheaper CN2 GT (Global Transit) tier.

What sets CN2 GIA apart is its dedicated, low-contention routing. Traffic runs on separate infrastructure from the public internet, so congestion elsewhere doesn’t bleed into your connection. The result is consistent latency, minimal jitter, and near-zero packet loss even during China’s evening peak window (roughly 20:00–23:00 CST).

The trade-off is cost. CN2 GIA plans have seen meaningful price increases in 2026, and the gap over commodity routing has widened. You’re paying for guaranteed quality — not just theoretical quality.

AS9929 Explained

AS9929 is China Unicom’s premium international backbone, sometimes referred to internally as the “A-Net.” It sits above China Unicom’s standard AS4837 network and uses optimized peering with dedicated capacity for international traffic.

In 2026, AS9929 has become the most compelling alternative to CN2 GIA for a wide range of use cases. The latency difference between the two is often negligible in practice, particularly on routes between Hong Kong or Japan and mainland China. Congestion resistance is strong — not quite at CN2 GIA’s level during the worst peak-hour scenarios, but close enough to matter.

The key advantage is price. AS9929 plans typically run 20–40% below equivalent CN2 GIA plans, making it the value pick for developers who want premium routing without the enterprise price tag.

AS4837 Explained

AS4837 is China Unicom’s standard international backbone — a commodity route, not a premium one. That distinction matters, but it doesn’t make AS4837 a bad choice. It’s built for volume, not for low-latency guarantees.

During off-peak hours, AS4837 performs respectably. Latency from Hong Kong or Tokyo to major Chinese cities is often acceptable. The problems surface during peak hours, when congestion drives latency spikes and packet loss high enough to make real-time applications unreliable.

Where AS4837 wins is raw bandwidth at low cost. If you’re transferring large files, running backups, or distributing video content where a few hundred extra milliseconds don’t matter, AS4837 delivers the most throughput per dollar.

Congestion Behavior During Peak Hours

Peak hours are where the real differences emerge. Between 20:00 and 23:00 CST, Chinese residential users flood the network with streaming, gaming, and social media traffic.

CN2 GIA’s dedicated infrastructure keeps it largely isolated from this congestion. Meaningful degradation is rare.

AS9929 handles peak hours well. It’s not immune to congestion, but the network is sized and prioritized to limit the impact. For most applications, the difference between AS9929 and CN2 GIA during peak hours is small enough that it won’t matter in practice.

AS4837 is where you’ll feel the squeeze. Peak-hour packet loss is a known, documented issue. For latency-sensitive workloads — VoIP, gaming servers, real-time APIs — this is a genuine problem. For bulk transfers or applications that tolerate retransmission, it’s manageable.

Pricing in 2026

CN2 GIA has gotten more expensive. Entry-level plans from established providers now start higher than they did two years ago, driven by infrastructure costs and sustained enterprise demand for the reliability guarantee.

AS9929 pricing has stayed more stable and currently represents the best value-to-performance ratio available. The gap between AS9929 and CN2 GIA has widened in AS9929’s favor.

AS4837 remains the cheapest option and is widely available. For high-bandwidth use cases where peak-hour performance isn’t critical, it’s hard to beat on a per-gigabyte basis.

Which Network Should You Choose?

Choose CN2 GIA if:

  • You’re running a production application where degraded performance has a direct business cost
  • Your users are in mainland China and real-time responsiveness matters — gaming, video calls, live commerce
  • You need consistent, SLA-level performance and reliability outweighs cost
  • You’re serving enterprise clients who expect stable, low-latency connections

Choose AS9929 if:

  • You want near-CN2-GIA performance without the premium price
  • You’re a developer or small team that needs solid China routing but can tolerate occasional minor peak-hour variance
  • You’re scaling across multiple nodes and need to balance performance with cost
  • The 2026 CN2 GIA price increases have pushed you to look for a credible alternative

Choose AS4837 if:

  • Your primary use case is high-bandwidth transfer where latency isn’t the main concern
  • You’re running backups, data sync, or content distribution to Chinese servers
  • Budget is tight and your application handles occasional packet loss gracefully
  • You’re in development or testing and don’t yet need production-grade routing

One more thing worth noting: some providers offer dual-stack or hybrid routing that switches networks based on traffic type or time of day. If your use case sits between categories, these setups are worth evaluating.

FAQs

What is the main difference between CN2 GIA and CN2 GT?

CN2 GIA is the premium, low-contention tier — dedicated routing, strong congestion resistance, consistent performance. CN2 GT is a mid-tier option that shares infrastructure with more traffic and is more susceptible to peak-hour degradation. GIA costs significantly more, but the stability difference is real.

Is AS9929 actually comparable to CN2 GIA in 2026?

For many use cases, yes. The latency gap has narrowed, and AS9929’s congestion handling has improved. Where CN2 GIA still has a clear edge is during severe peak-hour congestion, when its dedicated infrastructure provides more consistent protection. For most developers and small-to-medium applications, AS9929 delivers excellent performance at a lower cost.

Why has CN2 GIA pricing increased in 2026?

CN2 GIA pricing reflects the cost of maintaining dedicated, low-contention international capacity. Sustained enterprise demand and continued growth in China-facing applications have kept pricing pressure upward. The premium nature of the service means that trend is unlikely to reverse.

Can I use AS4837 for a gaming server targeting Chinese players?

It depends on the game and your tolerance for complaints. Off-peak, AS4837 can be acceptable. During peak hours (evenings CST), packet loss and latency spikes are common enough to affect gameplay noticeably. For a serious gaming deployment targeting Chinese players, AS9929 or CN2 GIA is the better call.

Does VPS location affect which network I should choose?

Yes, significantly. Hong Kong and Japan are the most common locations for China-optimized deployments because of their proximity. Singapore adds baseline latency regardless of network quality. Network type matters most, but location is the foundation — a CN2 GIA server in Singapore may underperform an AS9929 server in Hong Kong for Chinese users.

How do I verify which network a VPS provider is actually using?

Ask the provider directly and check their documentation. After provisioning, run traceroute tests to confirm the routing path — look for IP ranges associated with China Telecom (CN2) or China Unicom (AS9929/AS4837) in the output. Independent benchmark sites and community reviews can also help verify what providers claim.

Is AS9929 available from most major VPS providers?

Availability has grown in 2026, but AS9929 is still less common than AS4837. Providers with Hong Kong and Japan data centers are the most likely to offer it. Always confirm the network type before purchasing — marketing language like “premium routing” or “optimized China routing” doesn’t always mean AS9929 specifically.

Final Word

CN2 GIA, AS9929, and AS4837 each serve a real purpose. The mistake is defaulting to CN2 GIA simply because it’s the most recognized name. In 2026, AS9929 has earned its place as the practical choice for developers who need reliable China routing without paying enterprise prices for it.

Match the network to your actual requirements. If stability under load is non-negotiable, pay for CN2 GIA. If you want strong performance at a fair price, AS9929 is the pick. If bandwidth volume matters more than latency, AS4837 gets the job done.
