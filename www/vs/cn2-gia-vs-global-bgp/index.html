CN2 GIA vs Global BGP: Why Your China-Facing Site Is Slow

Quick answer

Standard Global BGP prioritizes cost over speed, creating bottlenecks at International Gateways during China’s peak hours. CN2 GIA (AS4809) runs on dedicated premium infrastructure that sidesteps this congestion entirely.

Bottom line:
Go with CN2 GIA if you need reliable sub-100ms latency to China around the clock. Stick with Global BGP only if budget constraints are severe and most users are outside Mainland China.

Choose CN2 GIA when

  • Your application can’t tolerate latency spikes (trading platforms, gaming, VoIP)
  • You want to avoid the notorious “Evening Peak” slowdown (6 PM - 11 PM GMT+8)
  • Chinese users expect local-speed performance for your business to succeed

Choose Global BGP when

  • You’re running everything through a CDN like Cloudflare where origin speed matters less
  • Keeping costs low trumps performance ($5/mo vs $20+/mo)
  • China represents a small fraction of your traffic, with most users in Southeast Asia, US, or Europe

What teams often misunderstand

The biggest myth: “Hong Kong servers = fast China access.” Wrong. Standard BGP from Hong Kong often bounces through the US or Europe before reaching China. Only direct routing or CN2 GIA guarantees the shortest path.

Understanding the Technical Differences

The core difference between CN2 GIA and Global BGP comes down to how your data travels to reach users in Mainland China.

Global BGP Routing

Standard BGP routes traffic along the “cheapest” path, which rarely means the fastest. Your Hong Kong or Singapore server might send data to Beijing like this:

Hong Kong → Tokyo → Los Angeles → San Francisco → Beijing

Telecom providers choose these routes to save money, not time. When peak hours hit, these international gateways get hammered, turning your snappy site into a crawling mess for Chinese users.

CN2 GIA Premium Network

CN2 GIA operates as AS4809, China Telecom’s premium network. Instead of that global ping-pong route, you get:

Hong Kong → Guangzhou → Beijing (Direct)

This network runs separate from regular internet traffic, maintaining steady performance when everyone else is fighting for bandwidth.

Real-World Performance Impact

The gap between these routing methods becomes painfully obvious during China’s internet rush hour.

Peak Hour Performance

From 6 PM to 11 PM GMT+8, when Chinese users flood online, standard BGP routes fall apart:

  • Standard BGP: Latency jumps to 50-300ms, timeouts become common
  • CN2 GIA: Holds steady at 30-80ms with minimal jitter

Geographic Variations

Different Chinese cities see varying impacts from your routing choice:

Tier 1 Cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen)

  • CN2 GIA: 20-50ms typical latency
  • Standard BGP: 100-400ms during peak hours

Tier 2/3 Cities

  • CN2 GIA: 40-80ms typical latency
  • Standard BGP: 200-600ms during peak hours

CN2 GIA’s advantage grows in smaller cities because it has better last-mile connections, while standard BGP gets stuck on overloaded regional networks.

  • Bounce rate jumps 32% when load time goes from 1s to 3s

If your China traffic brings in $10,000 monthly, the $15-40 premium for CN2 GIA pays for itself through better conversions alone.

Implementation Considerations

When moving to CN2 GIA, keep these technical factors in mind:

DNS Configuration Use GeoDNS to send Chinese users to your CN2 GIA servers while routing other regions through standard BGP infrastructure.

Load Balancing Set up health checks that understand China’s unique internet landscape.

Monitoring Deploy monitoring from multiple Chinese cities to track real performance across different ISPs.

Final recommendation

For any business serious about China in 2026, CN2 GIA isn’t optional—it’s essential. If your provider only offers standard BGP, your China conversion rates are probably taking a hit.

Your choice boils down to business priorities. If Chinese users drive significant revenue or represent key growth opportunities, CN2 GIA’s premium pays for itself through better user experience and higher conversions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between CN2 GT and CN2 GIA?

CN2 GT (Global Transit) uses CN2 infrastructure for part of the route, while CN2 GIA (Global Internet Access) runs premium routing end-to-end. CN2 GIA delivers better performance but costs more than CN2 GT.

Can I use a CDN instead of CN2 GIA for China traffic?

CDNs like Cloudflare help with static content but can’t fix routing problems for dynamic content or API calls. CN2 GIA improves the connection between your origin server and China, benefiting both cached and uncached requests.

How do I test if my current server has CN2 GIA routing?

Run a traceroute from your server to a Chinese IP address. Look for “59.43.x.x” IP addresses in the path—these indicate CN2 backbone usage. Online tools can also test latency from multiple Chinese cities.

Is CN2 GIA necessary for all types of websites?

CN2 GIA helps most with interactive applications, e-commerce sites, and real-time services. Static websites that CDNs can fully cache may see less benefit, though origin server performance still matters for cache misses.

Which Chinese ISPs work best with CN2 GIA?

CN2 GIA shows the biggest improvement for China Telecom users since it’s their premium network. China Unicom and China Mobile users also benefit, but gains may be smaller depending on specific routing agreements.

Can I switch from standard BGP to CN2 GIA without downtime?

Yes, set up a new CN2 GIA server and use DNS to gradually shift traffic. This lets you test performance before fully committing to the new infrastructure.

How does CN2 GIA perform during Chinese holidays or major events?

CN2 GIA stays more consistent during high-traffic periods like Chinese New Year or major shopping festivals because it runs on separate infrastructure from standard internet traffic.
